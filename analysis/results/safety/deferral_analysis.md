# Does defer-to-human actually work?

Session A. Generated by `analysis/operating_point.py`.

A clinic can only send so many scans to a human. So the question is not whether high-entropy cases are more often wrong. It is this: **if we defer the least confident X percent, does the tumour miss rate on the remaining scans actually drop?** Those are the patients nobody looks at twice.

Entropy cutoffs are quantiles of the internal validation split's entropy, applied to BRISC unchanged. Entropy in nats.

| deferral budget | entropy cutoff (nats) |
|---|---|
| 5% | 0.3234 |
| 10% | 0.0906 |
| 20% | 0.0378 |
| 30% | 0.0323 |

## BRISC, clean subset

With no deferral at all, the miss rate is **0.27%** (0.07 to 0.55), on 1476 tumours.

| target defer | actual defer | scans kept | miss rate on kept | 95% CI | misses caught by deferral |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5% | 13.1% | 2289 | 0.29% | 0.07 to 0.63 | 0 of 4 |
| 10% | 25.9% | 1951 | 0.23% | 0.00 to 0.54 | 1 of 4 |
| 20% | 41.2% | 1549 | 0.09% | 0.00 to 0.27 | 3 of 4 |
| 30% | 49.2% | 1337 | 0.10% | 0.00 to 0.32 | 3 of 4 |

## Internal held-out test, for comparison

No deferral: **1.10%** on 821 tumours.

| target defer | actual defer | miss rate on kept | misses caught |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% | 4.7% | 1.03% | 1 of 9 |
| 10% | 11.2% | 0.57% | 5 of 9 |
| 20% | 22.2% | 0.49% | 6 of 9 |
| 30% | 31.3% | 0.56% | 6 of 9 |

## Verdict

Deferring the least confident 20 percent catches **3 of 4** missed tumours and moves the miss rate on the remaining scans from 0.27% to 0.09%, a relative reduction of 67%.

Deferral works as intended here: the errors the model makes are concentrated in the cases it flags as uncertain, so escalating them removes most of the risk from the cases it answers on. That is the behaviour the safety argument depends on.

Figures: `figures/miss_rate_vs_defer_brisc.png`, `figures/miss_rate_vs_defer_internal_test.png`.

Caveat that applies to every number here: the BRISC clean subset excludes image-level duplicates of the training data but cannot exclude patient-level leakage, because neither dataset ships patient identifiers.
