# Are the heatmaps pointing at the tumour?

Session D, parts 2 and 3. Generated by `analysis/clinical_explainability.py`.

## The answer

**Mostly no.**

Measured against BRISC's radiologist-reviewed tumour masks, on the clean
subset (n=1476), does the single hottest pixel of the heatmap land inside
the actual tumour:

| path | pointing accuracy | 95% CI | chance | mass inside mask | lift over chance | mean best IoU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ResNet-50 Grad-CAM | **8.4%** | 7.1-9.9 | 1.7% | 3.0% | 1.87x | 0.064 |
| ViT-B/16 attention rollout | **41.0%** | 38.5-43.5 | 1.7% | 5.7% | 3.55x | 0.267 |

Both beat chance. Neither is good enough to point a clinician at a mass.

**Grad-CAM on ResNet-50 misses the tumour 9 times out of 10.** It puts 3.0% of
its activation inside the mask. It is not a tumour finder.

**Attention rollout on ViT is far better and still wrong most of the time.**
41% is a real signal, not noise. But a clinician told that this is where the
model looked would be looking in the wrong place 6 times out of 10.

## Where it breaks worst: small tumours

Localisation degrades exactly where a miss matters most.

| tumour size quartile | ResNet-50 | ViT |
|---|---|---|
| smallest | 2% | 23% |
| small | 5% | 39% |
| large | 10% | 49% |
| largest | 16% | 53% |

A small tumour is the one a human is most likely to miss unaided, so it is the
case where a heatmap would earn its place. It is the case where it works least.

## The finding that decides what the UI may say

**Localisation quality does not predict correctness. If anything it runs
backwards.**

| path | pointing when the model was RIGHT | when it was WRONG | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| ResNet-50 | 8.2% (n=1433) | 14.0% (n=43) | -5.7pp |
| ViT | 40.8% (n=1427) | 46.9% (n=49) | -6.2pp |

The heatmap is slightly MORE likely to land on the tumour when the model got
the answer wrong. The differences are small and the wrong-case samples are
small (43 and 49 images), so the honest reading is not that it is inverted. It
is that **the overlay carries no usable signal about whether to trust this
call**.

So the overlay cannot be sold as a confidence check. A clinician who trusts a
call because the heatmap looked sensible is not getting information. That is
manufactured trust, and `MISSION.md` forbids it.

## Part 3: faithfulness

### Model randomisation (Adebayo et al. 2018), both paths pass

| path | correlation with the map from a randomised model | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| ResNet-50 Grad-CAM | 0.046 | passes, the map depends on trained weights |
| ViT rollout | 0.338 | passes, the map depends on trained weights |

Correlation near 1 would mean the map draws image structure and ignores the
model. Neither does.

**This test was wrong on the first run and that first answer should be
discarded.** It randomised the classification head for both paths and reported
1.000 for ViT. Attention rollout never reads the classification head, so that
number was guaranteed by construction and measured nothing. Randomising the
encoder blocks, which rollout does read, gives 0.338.

### Deletion and insertion, and why they do not carry the verdict

| path | deletion AUC (lower better) | random | insertion AUC (higher better) | random |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ResNet-50 | 0.823 | 0.403 | 0.794 | 0.405 |
| ViT | 0.432 | 0.476 | 0.584 | 0.475 |

**These two disagree for ResNet-50, and the test is confounded, so they are
not the headline.** A real heatmap is spatially concentrated; a random one is
scattered across the whole image. Deleting a compact blob and deleting
scattered pixels damage an image in completely different ways, and the
scattered version destroys global texture the model depends on. The comparison
measures concentration as much as importance. Reported for completeness and
weighted accordingly. The pointing game against real radiologist masks is the
honest measurement and the verdict rests on it.

## Verdict for session C

**Keep the heatmap on the ViT path. Do not show it on ResNet-50.**

- ViT at 41% is weak but real, and the shipped config is ViT, so this is the
  path that matters.
- ResNet-50 Grad-CAM at 8.4% is close to decorative. Showing it would
  manufacture trust it has not earned.
- `HEATMAP_CAVEAT` was already right and stays, verbatim: *Shows where the
  model looked, not where the tumour is. Use it to catch obviously wrong
  calls, not to confirm right ones.*
- **Add no wording suggesting a sensible-looking heatmap means the call is more
  likely right.** It does not. That is measured above.
- Consider suppressing or further caveating the overlay on small tumours,
  where ViT drops to 23%, and on glioma and pituitary, where it drops to 25%.

## What this does not establish

- Masks cover tumour extent, not the evidence a radiologist would use. A
  heatmap sitting on peritumoral oedema just outside the mask scores as a miss
  and may not be wrong.
- The pointing game uses one pixel. It is standard and it is crude.
- This is one seed (42), on one dataset, whose masks nobody on this project
  has independently checked.
- **No clinician has looked at a single one of these overlays.** Whether a 41%
  hit rate helps or misleads a real reader under time pressure is not a
  question this file can answer.
